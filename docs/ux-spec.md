# UX spec

What every surface looks like, how it moves and what it does, with the measured value and its source for each. Decision 6 sets the rule: Arc's structure, behavior and timings, Dia's command bar and motion, and ilo's own look. [ux-parity.md](ux-parity.md) is the checklist built from this file, and [ux-principles.md](ux-principles.md) says why Arc's values are what they are. The last section is the plan for measuring Arc before it shuts down on 2026-12-22.

## Reading this spec

**Precedence.**

1. Decision 5 overrides everything else. A Space holds tabs, pinned tabs and a colour. A profile holds cookies, history, logins and extensions. Dia and Ardia merge the two, so where a Dia value below says "profile", read it as "Space".
2. Structure comes from Arc: what exists, where it sits, layout sizes and behavior. That covers the sidebar, Spaces, favorites, pinned and Today tabs, archive, split view, Peek, Little Arc and Boosts.
3. The command bar and motion come from Dia. Materials, colours, radii, type and icons are ilo's own (decision 6); a Dia or Arc look value below stands until its ilo-look issue replaces it. Dia's surfaces built around its assistant, such as its New Tab page, aren't copied (decision 6). Interactions that only Arc has (sidebar reveal, Clear, Peek, Little Arc) keep Arc's motion.
4. Where an Arc value hasn't been measured yet, the Dia value is the target and is marked *interim*. The Arc capture plan at the end lists what will replace it.
5. Values marked **Ardia** are Ardia's own choices, not measurements of Dia or Arc. Treat them as placeholders.

**Sources.** `D›X` is section X of Ardia's `docs/dia-spec.md`. `P§n` is section n of `docs/dia-feature-parity.md`. `O` is `docs/omnibox-ux.md`. Dia versions are 1.49.1, 1.50.1, 1.52 and 1.53.0. *bin* means read from Dia's or Arc's binary or asset catalogs, *cap* a 2× screen capture, *rec* a 60–240 fps recording. **Arc (Ardia)** marks Arc values Ardia recorded: Ardia's owner's Arc recordings and measurements of 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-22, a user's screenshot, and Arc's binary (Arc 1.167 for themes). **Chrome** means Chromium's own behavior. **Flecha** marks behavior read from Flecha's site and code at v0.1.0 (https://flecha.dev, MIT). **Helium** marks Helium's. **Ours** marks our decisions.

**Units.** Sizes are points. Times are ms or s. Colours are sRGB unless marked P3. "white 0.16" means white at alpha 0.16. Where two values appear, dark comes first, then light. Arc and Dia were measured in dark mode, so light values are their asset-catalog tokens and no light capture exists yet. Springs are given as response and damping ratio ζ (mass 1, stiffness (2π/response)², damping 4πζ/response). Curves are given as `cubic-bezier(x1, y1, x2, y2)` and a duration.

**Assets and names.** SF Symbol names identify a shape only. We draw our own icons, and they have to work off the Mac too. The names Little Arc, Top Apps and Dia's mark are references, not UI text. A feature named Peek is Arc's link preview. Ardia uses "peek" for the hidden sidebar sliding in, which this spec calls the *sidebar reveal*.

## Shared rules

### Window material (Dia 1.50.1 bin, D›Window translucency)

Layers, bottom to top. There is no grain: 1.50.1 measures 0.05–0.09 levels of noise.

| Layer | Dark | Light |
|---|---|---|
| Blur behind the window: emphasized, follows the window's active state | private material 29 | material 13 (`.hudWindow`) |
| Base tint, full opacity | black 0.4 | white 0.8 |
| Space gradient: the Space colour at the top, the same colour at HSL lightness + 0.25 (clamped) at the bottom, each stop at 0.36 (0.12 for a neutral colour) | in a layer at 0.5 | in a layer at 0.75 |
| Content card | #121212 0.5 | white 0.7 |

- Dark composite: `out = 0.18·tint_y + 0.82·0.6·blur`. Light: `out = 0.27·tint_y + 0.73·(0.8·255 + 0.2·blur)`. Here `tint_y` runs down the gradient.
- When the window is inactive, AppKit swaps the blur for an opaque fill tinted by the wallpaper. Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast do the same. Dia adds no fallback of its own. Compare captures only in the same key state.
- With no Space colour set, the gradient uses the system accent colour and follows its changes (Dia).
- Fitted Space colour (Dia's pink): P3 (177.6, 91.1, 106.8), which is #B25B6B in P3 and ≈ #BF556A in sRGB (HSL 349°, 0.36, 0.53). The other colours are unmeasured.

Material output measured over solid backdrops: black, sRGB grey levels 20 and 60, and white. Ports without AppKit materials approximate these (sRGB, active state):

| Material | Over black / grey 20 / grey 60 / white | Inactive fill |
|---|---|---|
| 29, dark | 24 / 32 / 45 / 104 | (52, 43, 41), tinted by the wallpaper |
| 29, light | 192 / 197 / 208 / 255 | 248 |
| 13, light | 137 / 146 / 165 / 243 | (239, 237, 236) |
| 13, dark | 33 / 44 / 72 / 182 | (58, 54, 53) |

- Ours (*interim*):
  - The window is light where its Space's theme asks for it or, automatic, where the system's appearance is light; a private window stays dark. Light is the table's light layers, material 13 under white 0.8, with text, glyphs and plates in Ink at dark's alphas. Where the window has to be opaque, light's grounds are Dia's composite over black and white, #E7E7E7 and #FDFDFD, and its stand-in is the composite over grey 20, #E9E9E9, until a check VM measures ours. The theme and icon pickers and tooltips stay dark over a light window.
  - A colour under HSL saturation 0.1 counts as neutral.
  - Where the window's look has to be opaque, it stands the material in with Dia's composite over grey 20 before the gradient, #131313, and puts the gradient over that. That's the reveal's panel over the page and a dragged row.
  - Base tint black 0.25, not 0.4. Material 29 reads darker in our window than in the table above: under black 0.4 our sidebar read 11 over black and 51 over white, the material at 18 and 85 against 24 and 104, two thirds of it in linear light at both ends. Under black 0.25 a check VM reads 23 / 27 / 34 / 70 over black / grey 20 / grey 60 / white, against Dia's 24 / 29 / 36 / 69. Ours is darker than Dia's over mid grey than at the ends, so no one tint puts all four within 2 levels; the `window` check fails past 3. The opaque fill of an inactive window and of Reduce Transparency is under the same lighter tint, unmeasured.

### Overlays

The command bar, the Space icon picker, menus, hover cards, Peek, find and every prompt over the page are child windows (decision 4). AGENTS.md › Over the page has the rules: a click outside closes the overlay and still does its own thing, and the host draws the overlay before showing it. Panels anchored to the URL field share one grid, except the command bar over the field, which keeps Arc's measures (Command bar › Over the URL field). **Ardia** set it on the owner's request; it is not measured from Dia:

- Content inset 16 on every side, corner radius 12. Rows 32 tall, with the hover fill inset 6 at radius 6.
- A 20 pt icon slot holds 13 pt glyphs, with text 10 pt after the slot, so titles start at 46. Separators sit 16 in, with 5 above and below.
- Header: the icon box is 16 from the top and left, with title and subtitle at 13/16 and 11/14 pt centred on it, and 8 pt below it.
- Placement: 8 from the card's side and 8 below the page's top. A panel taller than the page scrolls inside an 8 pt bottom margin.

### Toast

Arc's note at the window's top right, saying what was just done where the result can't be seen, such as a copy, even when the control used shows it too (Arc 1.170 rec at 2×, two Spaces deleted and ⇧⌘C; the URL field's Copy URL, Arc in use, the owner). It's a child window the mouse goes through.

- 46.5 tall, 8 inside the card's top right corner: 18 from the window's top and right edges. In a split view, 8 inside the focused pane's, as Arc shows a toast over its panel (Arc help), and it moves with the focus while it shows. Radius 10, with 0.5 pt of black 0.3 around it and a faint shadow below. Its fill goes down from the window's colour to darker (Arc's from (40,22,80) to (31,17,62) in a purple Space).
- White medium text, 12.5 in from its left and 13.5 from its right. A key's chip sits 8 from the words each side of it: 26 tall at radius 6, white 0.1, with the key semibold 9 in from each side.
- It comes and goes on Motion › Toast. After a Space is deleted it says "Space deleted! Use ⌘Z to undo." from about 0.1 s after the Space switch until 5 s after it. A new toast takes the words of one showing.
- Ours: any other edit that can be undone and isn't plain to see offers its Undo the same way, "Removed from Favorites", "Tab Moved to Space", "Folder Deleted", "Tabs Closed" (Close Tabs Below), "Deleted from Archive" and "Archive Cleared", with an Undo ⌘Z button after the words for 5 s, or as long as the pointer is over it. A click on the button is ⌘Z. The offer goes when the next edit has no toast of its own, and when the edit is undone or redone some other way.
- A tab opening in the Space shown while the sidebar is hidden, and its reveal isn't out, is told of, as its row can't be seen (Arc's release notes). It says Arc's "New Tab Created", or "3 New Tabs Created" for several at once, for 4 s or as long as the pointer is over it, and the whole toast is its button, as Arc's is clickable: a click on it brings the sidebar back at the top one's row, as Reveal Tab in Sidebar does. ### Keyboard

- Focus rules are in AGENTS.md › Keyboard with the page focused. Chrome's reserved commands reach the browser before the page sees them: New Tab, New Window, New Incognito Window, Close Tab, Close Window, Reopen Closed Tab, Next/Previous Tab and Quit. Other browser keys reach the browser only if the page doesn't keep them. A page keeps ⌘F, for example. Pressed again within a second of one the page kept, the key runs the browser's command, as Arc's second press does; a key repeating as it's held isn't a press (the Mac tells; Linux and Windows don't yet, so a held key counts there). Per command, Settings › Shortcuts has Prefer Websites, the default, or Prefer ilo, which sends that command's keys to the browser before the page, as Arc's Prefer Arc does (Arc 1.170 Settings). Arc's Notify me when shortcuts are conflicting isn't built (owner call, 2026-11-01: the second press works without it, and a toast on every kept key would nag). Extensions can't take the reserved keys (Chrome).
- Off the Mac (Linux, Windows) every key is pressed with Ctrl for ⌘ and Alt for ⌃: ⌘T is Ctrl+T, ⌃2 is Alt+2, ⌃⇧= is Alt+Shift+=, ⌥⌘↓ is Ctrl+Alt+↓. - **Distance ≥ 0.055 (colourful).** The tile is white 0.20 (dark) or white (light). Over it goes the icon at 22 % opacity, blurred with radius 5 and aspect-filled to 2.5× the tile. The ring is a 3 pt border showing the same blurred icon over black (dark: at 75 %, saturation 2, brightness −0.1) or white (light: at 100 %).
- **Distance < 0.055 (one colour).** The tile is filled with the mean colour, the icon is drawn as a white template, and the ring is white with soft-light compositing. A near-white icon (relative luminance > 0.88) gets a black fill and a white 0.30 ring instead.
- **No usable icon.** Fill black (dark) or white (light), with the default selection ring, white 0.45, 0.5 pt.
- A secondary selection (multi-select) gets the secondary fill and no ring. Emoji icons draw at 16 pt and are themed the same way.

Ours (dark only, `app/src/shell/tile_theme.rs`): the theme is made from the favicon's pixels the first time its tile is drawn selected, and kept until the favicon changes. Where it differs from Dia's:

- GPUI has no blur or blend mode, so the blur is a Gaussian of sigma 5 on the icon at ≤ 32 px, cropped to the icon's own extent with nothing beyond it (Dia's crop isn't known), and the ring is an image baked for an 88 pt tile and stretched between its corners to the tile's width. Its saturation and brightness are applied in the image.
- The soft-light ring is the fill's colour with white soft-lit over it (W3C's formula), drawn as a plain border, which is the same where the fill is opaque. All themed rings are 3 pt (Dia's `itemStrokeWidth`; the one-colour and near-white rings' width is assumed).
- An emoji icon has no theme yet and shows as no usable icon: GPUI can't give the pixels of text, so it needs the core to hand over the emoji as an image.
- Light mode: not themed. A light window's selected tile is Dia's white one with the default ring, whatever its icon, until the light theme is built.

### Spaces

**Model** (decision 5)

- A Space has a name, a colour, its pinned tabs, its Today tabs and a profile.

**Inline completion** (Chrome; Dia rec, O)

- It completes only one term, with the caret at the end.
- It never completes during IME composition, and ↩ then commits the composition instead of navigating. It completes again after the composition is committed.
- It never completes after a deletion (⌫, ⌦, ⌥⌫, ⌘⌫ or cut) until the next typed character. After a deletion, the search row goes first.
- It never completes right after a paste, or when the input ends in whitespace.
- Hosts complete first; paths only when the typed text contains "/". The typed prefix keeps its case.
- Only hosts the user has typed are eligible: typed once for a host, twice for a deeper URL. Bookmark-only and open-tab-only hosts are listed but don't inline (Chrome).
- → or ⌘→ accepts the completion, and so does a click at its end. A ⌫ after accepting deletes one character and doesn't complete again.

### Page dialogs

*Ours*: Arc's page dialogs aren't recorded, so these follow the principles and the dialog around them. What a page asks for itself, and what Chrome asks for a page, is the browser's dialog (Spaces › Renaming and deleting) over the tab's card, in place of Chrome's views, which have no window here.

- **Where.** The dim covers the tab's card alone, with its corners, and the card sits centred on it, so the dialog has the tab's place (Everything has a place) and the sidebar stays live: another tab, another Space and other windows can be chosen. It's tab-modal: it goes, with the dialog's motion, when another tab shows, and comes back when its tab does. Only the window in front shows it; another window's waits for it to come to the front. A click on the dim does nothing, as the page waits for an answer. It waits behind the browser's own dialog and Chrome's other questions, such as adding an extension.
- **Words.** Chrome's, with Chrome's title, "<origin> says", where Chrome has one. The buttons carry ESC and ↩ chips (Teach the keys where they're used). The action is in the Space's accent, as an answer to a page takes nothing back. While an input method composes in a field, ↩ is its.
- **Chrome's rules.** Chrome decides when a page may ask and what a dialog becomes as its tab hides or goes to another page: an alert from a tab that isn't shown lets the page go on at once and shows when the tab does, and a confirm or prompt from one is dropped; a tab that hides lets its alert's page go on, and the alert comes back with the tab, but cancels its confirm or prompt; a new dialog takes the place of the last, and going to another page takes it away. A page that asks in a loop is stopped as Chrome's is, by closing its tab, which the live sidebar allows; Chrome gave up the suppress checkbox for these.
- **alert, confirm and prompt.** The title, then the page's message as the detail. An alert has OK alone, confirm OK and Cancel, prompt a field under the message, holding the page's default, selected. ↩ is OK and Esc Cancel.
- **Leave site?** A page whose beforeunload handler asks, as the user goes from it, reloads it or closes its tab or window, asks in Chrome's words: "Leave site?" or "Reload site?" over "Changes you made may not be saved.", with Stay, which Chrome calls Cancel, and Leave or Reload in red, as leaving loses what was typed. ↩ leaves and Esc stays, and staying also stops a quit under way. Chrome asks these one at a time for the whole app, so a tab no window shows, such as one closed from its row, comes to the front window to ask. A page that asks again soon after gets Chrome's "Prevent this page from creating additional dialogs", and with it ticked it can't hold the user again.
- **Sign in.** A site's HTTP sign-in, basic or digest, asks in Chrome's words: "Sign in", the site's origin, and "Your connection to this site is not private" under it if it's plain HTTP, with a Username and a Password field, Cancel and Sign in. The username field has the keyboard, Tab goes to the password, which shows dots and won't copy, and ↩ signs in. It waits with its tab, as an alert does, and Chrome takes it away once another tab signs in to the same realm. Esc shows the page the site sent without signing in. A saved password isn't filled in yet (*interim*).
- **Open another app.** A link whose scheme another app handles, such as zoommtg: or maps:, asks in Chrome's words: "Open Maps?" over "<origin> wants to open this application.", with Cancel and Open Maps. Here ↩ is Cancel's and the action takes a click, as Chrome's dialog defaults to Cancel, so a key press the page timed can't open an app. From a secure origin it offers Chrome's "Always allow <origin> to open links of this type in the associated app", kept in the profile's prefs as Chrome keeps it, and a site going to another goes with its question. mailto: opens the mail app without asking, as in Chrome.
- **Beside the question and the note.** A page's question and the protected-video note hang under the URL field, outside the dim, so they stay as they are while a dialog covers the card, and each is answered on its own. A tab showing its page in Safari's engine holds back the dialog its Chromium page asked until the tab is back in Chromium. A page in Safari's engine has no dialogs of its own yet: WebKit drops its alert, confirm and prompt (*interim*).
- **Agents.** Agents get nothing: no tool sees or answers a page's dialog, which goes to the user in the Agent Space as anywhere. A sign-in in an agent's tab is the user's to give, so it's a handoff by its nature: the agent's page waits until the user signs in or cancels. A page in an agent's Space opens no other app, whoever clicked, and asks nothing, even where the user ticked "Always allow": agents act inside the browser alone. Nothing typed in a dialog is logged.

  - The user's preference, so agents have no call to change it, as with the favorites' scope.
  - Haptics (decided by the owner, 2026-10-29): the trackpad gives one detent as a drag reaches a stop, a handle the field's rim, the intensity knob either end or the dial the top, and none while it's held there or as it moves freely. It follows Settings' haptics switch, as the Space swipe's detent does, and Linux and Windows have none.
- Measure Arc's swatch clicks, its < and >, −, an appearance button, a secondary handle dragged alone, the picker from the Space's menu, and the keyboard in the picker.

### Mini Player

Arc calls its Picture in Picture the Mini Player. Its values are Dia's (Dia 1.50.1 rec, D›Picture in Picture) until Arc's are measured.

- **Turning it off.** Arc's Site Controls have a Picture in Picture switch for the site, and its Advanced settings turn it off everywhere. Ours is Chrome's automatic picture-in-picture: a page that asks for it with its media session floats as its tab hides: a video playing with sound on a site the user plays media on often, or a call. Site Controls list the site's Automatic picture-in-picture once the page has asked, with Allow, Block and Ask, Ask floating with Chrome's question on the window. Settings › Advanced's switch blocks it by default in every profile, so a site allowed in its Site Controls still floats; turned on, the default is Chrome's again. Arc's Mini Player menu, which turns it off for the site for a day or for good, waits for the Mini Player to be ours.
- **Moving, sizing and going back** (Arc's; how far is ours, *interim*). A video's Mini Player moves with a scroll over it, as far as the content would go, and with a drag anywhere off its controls; ⌘ with a scroll (Ctrl off the Mac) or a pinch sizes it about its middle, 1.1 × a wheel notch, keeping its shape, within Chrome's least and greatest. Its corners and edges resize it as Chrome's do, and it stays on the display it's mostly on. A double-click off its controls goes back to its tab, as its back-to-tab button does: the window holding the tab shows it, in its Space. AppKit hands Chromium's windows no pinch yet, so on the Mac it's ⌘-scroll. A call's Mini Player is a page of its own, which scrolls as a page does and moves by its top, as Chrome's.
- **Its keys** (Chrome's, *interim*). With the Mini Player key, Space plays or pauses and ← and → seek 10 s, and the keyboard's media keys play or pause it, as Chrome's do; a page's media session gives it previous and next track. Arc's back and forward 15 s wait for buttons of ours. The playback speed is the page's, which the Mini Player keeps.

- At rest: the video only, with 6 pt circular corners, a 1 pt rim of white 0.17 inside the edge and a soft shadow. It appears ≈ 125 ms after the tab switch with no animation.
- On hover: a black 0.35 scrim fades in over 200 ms, ease-in-out. Back-to-tab (28 × 28) and close (29 × 28) buttons sit 12 from the top and their sides, radius ≈ 6, white 0.09 (≈ 0.13 under the pointer). The origin shows in 13 pt, centred, baseline 30. Pause is two 7 × 42 capsules 10 apart. A 5 pt progress bar runs from 12 to width − 12, 9 above the bottom; the played part is white, the rest white ≈ 0.27.
- Close fades it out in ≈ 100 ms and the video keeps playing in its tab. Back to tab fades it out in ≈ 70 ms.
- Throw (Dia 1.53.0 bin): a release faster than 350 pt/s (or a last swipe delta over 6 pt) throws it to the screen corner within 45°. It lands 10 pt in, on a spring of 0.9 s / ζ 0.9 that starts at the throw velocity. A slow release stays where it is let go.

### Boosts

Arc kept Boosts in its account and shared them in a gallery. Ours are local files with no account (principle 2).

- **What a Boost is** (Arc). CSS and a script the user writes for one site, plus Zap: click an element to hide it. Measure Arc's editor, Zap, and where a Boost starts from.
- **Storage** (Flecha). A folder of plain files, one Boost per site, so the user can keep them in git.
- **Applying** (Flecha). CSS changes apply live; a script change applies on the page's next load.
- **Seeing and turning off.** Site Controls lists the site's Boosts, each with an off switch, and Settings turns them all off. Arc's look: measure.
- **Agents.** A Boost script runs inside the user's logged-in pages, where it can read what principle 3 keeps from agents. Agents get no path to read, write or turn on a Boost (decision 8). Any local program can write to the folder, a coding agent included, so a script that changed outside the browser stays off until the user turns it on again in the browser.

### Ad blocking

Helium and Flecha block ads and trackers by default; Arc and Dia don't. Ours does from a new install's first page, with nothing to install (Phase 3).

- **Default.** On in every profile, new and existing, and in private windows, on every site. No site is trusted to show its ads. Arc's uBlock Origin trusted YouTube; ours blocks there as anywhere, and Site Controls turn it off for YouTube as for any site (owner, 2026-10-29).
- **Engine.** Upstream uBlock Origin (gorhill's), pinned by version and sha256 in `app/ublock.ini`, with our key added to its manifest for a stable id, and its housekeeping timers made to run once after the work that needs them instead of re-arming, so they don't wake an idle browser (`app/fetch-ublock.sh`). Not Helium's fork: upstream's releases are the ones its users and list authors test. It's a Manifest V2 extension; ungoogled-chromium's patch keeps Manifest V2 on 155 and still on 156.0.8078.47-1. `app/build.sh` puts it in the framework's resources, and the core loads it into each profile at every launch, as `--load-extension` does: it updates with the browser, never from a store, and nothing about it is saved but its own storage. It's in the Extensions menu and can be pinned, as any extension; its action's menu has no Remove, since Settings turns it off.
- **Settings for it.** The core gives uBlock Origin its settings as managed storage, a policy under the platform's, so an administrator's policy still wins: its lists from the source below, and checked weekly. In uBlock Origin itself, only its housekeeping timers change (Engine).
- **Turning it off.** Settings › General's Block ads and trackers turns it off in every profile; the browser keeps it with the Spaces. Site Controls' first row, Block ads and trackers, with Lucide's shield, says On or Off for this site and turns it the other way on a click, reloading the page so what it held back loads. It turns it off for the registrable domain (the host, for an IP address or localhost), kept in the profile, and in the profile's private windows, where the row isn't shown. The row isn't there for a page that isn't http(s), or with it off in Settings. Settings › General lists every site it's off on, every loaded profile's, in the order they were turned off, each a row with the site, its profile's name in grey when there's more than one profile, and a Turn On button, which lets it block there again without reloading a page open there; Settings' section goes when there are none (ours). uBlock Origin's own popup keeps its power button.
- **Filter lists.** uBlock Origin ships its lists, so the first launch blocks without the network. It checks them 37 s after a launch if they're out of date, then once a week while the browser runs, never more often (principle 1). Their source is a default service URL, `kFilterListsSource` in `core/ad_block.cc`, a forks' own `assets.json`; empty, it's the one uBlock Origin ships, naming its upstream hosts (principle 2).
- **First launch.** uBlock Origin lets requests through until it has read its lists, about 3 s on a new profile's first launch. Its own fix, holding requests until then, would hold the launch's first page too, so it stays off until the cost is measured against the launch gate.
- **Cost.** Page-load and memory numbers, with and without it, against the performance gate.
- **Agents.** They get no call that changes it, everywhere or for a site: it keeps trackers off the user's own pages (decision 8). The core refuses an agent's `ad_blocking` action, with why, in the activity log (Agents UI). An agent's pages are blocked as the user's are.

### Environments

From Flecha: one project's origins, such as local, staging and production.

- **Setup.** The user adds, edits and removes a project's origins in Settings.
- **Switching** (Flecha). ⌥⌘E (*interim*; check for clashes) opens the same page on the project's next origin. Only the origin changes; the path, query and hash stay. The command bar lists the page's other origins.
- **Badge** (Flecha). The URL field names the environment of the page's origin. Production is red, so it's never mistaken for a safe place to click around.

### Dock dot

The green dot on ilo's Dock icon takes the color of the key window's Space, so the Dock says which Space you're in. A Space with no color, or a private window, shows the green.

- **Native look.** The dot is recolored on macOS's own drawing of the icon, so it keeps the style's glass. In the Tinted and Clear icon styles, the icon stays exactly as macOS draws it: a colored dot would be the one icon out of style in the Dock.
- **Never in the switch.** One swap takes the main thread 10–45 ms, more than the 16 ms a whole Space switch may take. The dot changes 400 ms after the last switch, once the slide has settled, so a run of quick switches is one swap.
- **Launch.** The icon takes the color it quit with while the Dock still bounces.
